# 009.2 — Test Build Manifest

This document specifies the unit that [009.1](009.1-Performance-Characterization-Protocol.md) is run on:
what it contains, what it deliberately leaves out, the order it is assembled in, and which open item each
stage answers. It **selects** from the bill of materials rather than restating it — each stage names the
[007](007-Bill-of-Materials.md) subassembly it draws and states only what differs from a baseline build.

## The minimum build is nearly the whole robot

Reach, repeatability, payload and speed are properties of the assembled arm: they cannot be measured on a
subassembly, a rig, or a partial kinematic chain. Of everything
[009.1](009.1-Performance-Characterization-Protocol.md) measures, only the fan rail and the two
during-assembly readings come off partial hardware. **The test build is therefore the baseline build minus
the exclusions below**, staged so that each stage answers what it can before the next one is committed.

What the staging buys is not a smaller build but an earlier answer: a board that does not drive a motor is
worth discovering before 109 parts are printed, and a belt ordered before its centre distance is measured
is ordered twice.

## Gate items — close these before cutting anything

Four items are prerequisites rather than outputs. Three are authoring work with no hardware involved, and
the build cannot be completed or calibrated without them.

| Gate | Why it gates | If it is not closed first |
|---|---|---|
| [DC-10](009-Design-Completion.md#from-scratch-calibration-files) — two calibration job wrappers | [006 §Steps 2–3](006-Firmware-and-Calibration.md#factory-calibration-procedure) drive the calibration engine through these two files | No calibration, therefore no trustworthy encoder counts. [009.1](009.1-Performance-Characterization-Protocol.md) §§ 1.3, 2.1 and 4 cannot run at all |
| [DC-12](009-Design-Completion.md#wrist-pulley-rework) — five re-cut parts | The models carry the previous version's tooth counts while `AxisCal` carries the specified ratio ([006](006-Firmware-and-Calibration.md#drive-constants-axiscal)) | Every commanded J4 and J5 angle is scaled by 2.4. Printing the set as it stands wastes five parts and every wrist measurement taken on them |
| [DC-11(h)](009-Design-Completion.md#procurement-data) — the L3-to-differential joint | Nothing in the model set bridges the elbow tube to the wrist, so the arm has no structural path across that gap | **The arm cannot be closed between the elbow and the wrist.** There is no part to print, and no substitute is specified |
| [DC-14](009-Design-Completion.md#power-inlet-mounting) — the inlet's mounting home | The receptacle is panel-mount and the design has no panel | Deferrable only: the test build takes bench leads to `J24` instead (Stage 1). The test build does not close this item |

⚠️ **DC-11(h) is the hard one.** DC-10 and DC-12 are work with a known shape — reconstruct two job
wrappers, re-cut five tooth bands. The joint between the L3 tube and the differential has to be designed
from the two faces it lands on, and until it exists there is no complete arm to characterize.

**A fallback that defers DC-12, at a cost.** [008.6](008-Assembly.md#0086-differential) offers
`config="previous"`, which builds the previous version's proven differential. Run against the matching
`AxisCal`, it yields a working arm without the re-cut parts — but J4 and J5 resolution, speed and ratio are
then measured on a wrist that is not the design of record, and those measurements do not transfer.
Reach, base rigidity, L1 and the harness checks do transfer. Take this path only to get the rest of the
protocol moving while DC-12 is authored.

## Stage 0 — Desk work, procurement, print tuning

Nothing here needs a robot, and everything downstream waits on it.

1. **Close the three authoring gates** above. DC-14 stays open and is worked around.
2. **Order the strain-wave sets on day one.** Three sets of [C-201](007.1-Parts-Catalog.md#c-201--521-strain-wave-component-set)
   are the longest-lead purchase in the build by a wide margin
   ([009 § Strain-wave component set](009-Design-Completion.md#strain-wave-component-set)); every other bought
   part can be had inside a week. Ordering them last turns a two-month build into a five-month one.
3. **Start board fabrication.** The Motor Control Board and the five optical boards
   ([005 § Boards](005-Electronics-and-Control.md#boards)) are fabricated from the gerbers in the hardware
   repository, not bought. Order **spare optical boards** — they are small and cheap, and one dead board
   with the arm closed up costs a joint and a teardown.
4. **Buy the remainder** of [007.1](007.1-Parts-Catalog.md), less the two gated purchases below.
5. **Tune print parameters against a fit coupon before committing the part set.**
   [007.2 § Material](007.2-Printed-Parts.md#material) records that layer height, wall count, infill and
   orientation were never published, and the fits that matter are interference fits. Print a coupon
   carrying one 6703 bearing seat and one 4.4 × 1.5 mm strake slot, adjust until the bearing presses in
   without splitting the boss and the strake beds on adhesive without rattling, then hold those settings
   for the whole run and record them against [DC-11(d)](009-Design-Completion.md#procurement-data). Two
   coupons cost an hour; a mis-set wall count costs the print list twice.

### Purchases deliberately held back

| Held | Released by | Why it cannot be ordered yet |
|---|---|---|
| Wrist and elbow belts | Stage 4, via [009.1 § Test 2.2](009.1-Performance-Characterization-Protocol.md#test-22-belt-centre-distances) | Belt length follows from the centre distance the built arm actually presents, and the specified lengths are provisional. Measure, then order |
| The cooling fan and its bracket, `#652-001` / `#800-005` | Stage 1, via [009.1 § Section 5](009.1-Performance-Characterization-Protocol.md#section-5-fan-rail-measurement) | The fan's voltage rating cannot be chosen before the rail behind `J23` is measured ([DC-11(b)](009-Design-Completion.md#procurement-data)) |

## Stage 1 — Bench electronics

The whole control chain, on the bench, with no mechanics attached.

**Draws:** Motor Control Board, FPGA module and one optical board with its emitter and detector
([005 § Boards](005-Electronics-and-Control.md#boards)); the DC supply; one stepper; one printed code disk
to pass through the optical gap; bench leads to `J24`.

**Assemble:** fit exactly one opto-supply jumper. Deploy the firmware and set the serial number per
[006 § Step 1](006-Firmware-and-Calibration.md#step-1--deploy-files-and-set-serial-number). Bring the
board up under the power-on test in [009 § Motor Control PCB](009-Design-Completion.md#motor-control-pcb).

**Answers:** the board-level part of [DC-7](009-Design-Completion.md#motor-control-pcb) — jumper state and
rail voltages — and [Section 5](009.1-Performance-Characterization-Protocol.md#section-5-fan-rail-measurement),
which releases the fan purchase. The per-joint half of DC-7 waits for Stage 5.

**Exit gate:** every driver channel steps the test motor when the motor is moved to that channel's
connector; the boost and buck rails hold their specified values ([005 § Power](005-Electronics-and-Control.md#power));
the optical channel's output changes as the code disk passes; the fan is chosen and ordered.

⚠️ Do not skip this stage on the argument that the board is a known design. It is a **fabricated** board
from recovered gerbers, and this is the only point in the build where it is accessible from both sides.

## Stage 2 — Base and shoulder stack

**Draws:** [007.2 Base](007-Bill-of-Materials.md#0072-base),
[007.3 Harmonic Drive Motors](007-Bill-of-Materials.md#0073-harmonic-drive-motors),
[007.4 Main Pivot](007-Bill-of-Materials.md#0074-main-pivot), and the base mounting plate stock
[C-509](007.1-Parts-Catalog.md#c-509--base-mounting-plate-stock).

**Assemble:** build the glue rigs ([008.1](008-Assembly.md#0081-glue-rig-assembly)) first — every bonded
carbon-fibre member in the robot passes through them — then
[008.2](008-Assembly.md#0082-base), [008.3](008-Assembly.md#0083-harmonic-drive-motors) and
[008.4](008-Assembly.md#0084-main-pivot).

**Answers:**

- [DC-13](009-Design-Completion.md#base-height-and-l1) on two fronts: the assembled stack gives the
  mounting-face-to-J2-axis dimension via [Test 3.1](009.1-Performance-Characterization-Protocol.md#test-31-mounting-face-to-j2-axis),
  and [008.4](008-Assembly.md#0084-main-pivot) settles how many clamps the pivot in fact consumes.
- [DC-11(a)](009-Design-Completion.md#procurement-data) — the stepper's identity is confirmed or refuted
  the moment one is offered up to a printed motor end cap. A motor that does not seat is discovered here
  or it is discovered with three of them buried in the arm.
- [DC-11(d)](009-Design-Completion.md#procurement-data) — the first substantial print run either holds the
  Stage 0 coupon's fits at scale or it does not.

**Exit gate:** L1 recorded; clamp count recorded; J1, J2 and J3 each turn by hand across their travel with
no binding and no detectable output-side lash beyond what the strain-wave set contributes.

## Stage 3 — Differential subassembly

Built and checked on the bench, off the arm, where the wrist is still reachable from every side.

**Draws:** [007.6 Differential](007-Bill-of-Materials.md#0076-differential) in full, including the
fabricated CF strakes and rod, the thrust bearing stack and the finishing-nail locking dowels.

**Assemble:** [008.6](008-Assembly.md#0086-differential).

**Answers:**

- [Test 1.1](009.1-Performance-Characterization-Protocol.md#test-11-j4-and-j5-travel) by hand, before the
  wrist is ever driven. A differential that cannot reach its specified travel is a mechanical fault, and
  finding it now costs one subassembly rather than a stripped arm.
- [DC-11(e)](009-Design-Completion.md#procurement-data) — assembling from the full parts list is what
  determines whether every modelled part has somewhere to go. Note any part the procedure never calls for,
  and any step that calls for a part the list does not carry.

**Exit gate:** J4 and J5 each sweep their specified travel; the parts list is fully consumed or every
leftover is accounted for; no part was forced.

## Stage 4 — Arm through to the wrist, dry

The arm is closed structurally but **no belts are fitted** — their length is not known yet.

**Draws:** [007.5 Arm Body](007-Bill-of-Materials.md#0075-arm-body),
[007.7 End Arm Hub](007-Bill-of-Materials.md#0077-end-arm-hub),
[007.8 External Gear](007-Bill-of-Materials.md#0078-external-gear),
[007.9 External Gear Mount + Differential Motors](007-Bill-of-Materials.md#0079-external-gear-mount--differential-motors),
plus the part authored to close [DC-11(h)](009-Design-Completion.md#procurement-data).

**Assemble:** [008.5](008-Assembly.md#0085-arm-body), [008.7](008-Assembly.md#0087-end-arm-hub),
[008.8](008-Assembly.md#0088-external-gear).

**Answers:** [Test 2.2](009.1-Performance-Characterization-Protocol.md#test-22-belt-centre-distances),
which releases the belt order, and [DC-11(c)](009-Design-Completion.md#procurement-data) — the belt
directors either accept the specified idler type or they do not, and fitting them is the test.

**Exit gate:** both centre distances recorded and the belts ordered. Expect a wait here; it is the reason
Test 2.2 sits this early in the protocol rather than alongside the other belt work.

## Stage 5 — Belts, harness, tool interface

**Draws:** the belts ordered at Stage 4; [007.10 Wire Harness](007-Bill-of-Materials.md#00710-wire-harness);
[007.11 Tool Interface / Gripper](007-Bill-of-Materials.md#00711-tool-interface--gripper); the fan and
bracket ordered at Stage 1.

**Assemble:** [008.9](008-Assembly.md#0089-belts), [008.10](008-Assembly.md#00810-wire-harness),
[008.11](008-Assembly.md#00811-tool-interface--gripper).

**Answers:** the per-joint half of [DC-7](009-Design-Completion.md#motor-control-pcb) — with the harness
landed, each joint can be commanded and watched for its own encoder responding. Run
[Test 1.2](009.1-Performance-Characterization-Protocol.md#test-12-tool-conductors-through-the-wrist) while
the tool conductors are being dressed through the wrist, and repeat it at Stage 6 with the wrist driven
rather than turned by hand.

**Exit gate:** all five arm joints step under firmware command and each reports motion on its own encoder;
six conductors are continuous from the board to the tool connector; both tool servos answer on the bus.

## Stage 6 — Calibration, then characterization

**Assemble:** nothing further. Run the factory calibration procedure end to end
([006](006-Firmware-and-Calibration.md#factory-calibration-procedure),
[008.12](008-Assembly.md#00812-firmware-configuration-and-calibration)). Steps 2 and 3 consume the two
files DC-10 produces; without them the build stops here with a mechanically complete but uncalibrated arm.

**Answers:** everything remaining in [009.1](009.1-Performance-Characterization-Protocol.md) — the code-disk
check, L4 from Cartesian residual, base rigidity, and the whole performance envelope. These are the
measurements that need a *calibrated* arm, which is why they are last and why no amount of staging can
pull them earlier.

**Exit gate:** [009.1 § Section 7](009.1-Performance-Characterization-Protocol.md#section-7-closing-out) is
worked through and each result is written into the document that owns it.

## What the test build leaves out

The baseline build already excludes cosmetic covers and alternate end effectors — see
[007.2 § Not in the build list](007.2-Printed-Parts.md#not-in-the-build-list). The test build omits two
further things:

| Omitted | Reason |
|---|---|
| Wire Entry, `#800-001` / `#800-002` | No assembly step places them and their role depends on how [DC-14](009-Design-Completion.md#power-inlet-mounting) resolves. Printing them now risks printing the wrong part; the test build takes power in on bench leads instead |
| Gripper fingers and grip pads | The tool-bus and conductor checks exercise the interface body and the roll and span axes. The fingers add nothing a measurement depends on, and can be built later without disturbing anything |

Everything else in [007](007-Bill-of-Materials.md) is required. Resist trimming further: a subassembly
left out of a characterization build silently changes the mass, the stiffness, or the harness routing that
the measurement is supposed to capture.

## Additions that exist only for the test build

| Addition | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Bench supply on leads direct to `J24` | Stands in for the unmounted power inlet. Deliver the supply specified in [005 § Power](005-Electronics-and-Control.md#power) — the substitution is mechanical, not electrical |
| Print fit coupon | Stage 0 print-parameter tuning. Not a robot part and not printed again afterwards |
| Spare optical boards | One failure otherwise costs a teardown |
| Instruments | Listed in [009.1 § Instruments](009.1-Performance-Characterization-Protocol.md#instruments) |

## Recording what the build tells you

[009.1 § Section 6](009.1-Performance-Characterization-Protocol.md#section-6-recording-template) carries the
template for measured values, and [§ Section 7](009.1-Performance-Characterization-Protocol.md#section-7-closing-out)
routes each one to the document that owns it.

Stages 0 through 5 additionally produce findings that are not measurements — a motor that seated or did
not, a part with nowhere to go, the print settings that held. **Write these down at the bench, against the
DC item in the table above, as they happen.** They are the answer to roughly half the open items in
[009](009-Design-Completion.md), they exist only as an observation at the moment they occur, and a stage
later the evidence is inside a closed assembly.
