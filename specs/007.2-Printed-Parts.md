## Material

**Onyx (Markforged)** is the material of record for this generation of the design — the previous
generation printed in PLA, HD and later in Onyx. Parts whose model filename carries **"CF"** are printed
with **continuous carbon fibre reinforcement** (Markforged Mark Two class), not plain Onyx.

The printed parts are **scaffolding, not primary structure** — carbon fibre strakes and tubes
([007.1 §5](007.1-Parts-Catalog.md#5-structural-composites-and-metal-stock)) carry load between joints, and
the printed bodies locate them ([004](004-Mechanical-Architecture.md)). Two consequences bound any material
substitution:

- **Printed threads and thin captures strip easily.** [004](004-Mechanical-Architecture.md) limits torque
  throughout for this reason. A weaker filament narrows an already narrow margin.
- **The [base mounting plate](007.1-Parts-Catalog.md#c-509--base-mounting-plate-stock) must not be
  printed** in any material — it is the one load-bearing metal part in the robot.

### Print profile

The original parts were produced on Markforged equipment with settings that were never published, so the
profile below is **derived from the features the parts have to hold** rather than recovered. Each parameter
states the geometry that sets it, so a builder on different equipment can re-derive it rather than copy it.

| Parameter | Value | What sets it |
|---|---|---|
| Nozzle | **0.40 mm or finer** | The narrowest land in the set is between encoder slots: `#730-002`'s J4 track runs 0.800 mm slots on a 360/115 pitch from r 24.500, leaving **0.539 mm** of material at the inner radius — 1.35 extrusion widths at 0.40 mm. Slice the code disks and `#730-002` with variable-width perimeters, or fit a 0.25–0.30 mm nozzle for them |
| Layer height | **0.125 mm**, or 0.100 mm | The thinnest axial feature in the set is the [Belt Director](#arm-body-and-belt-directors--0075)'s 0.500 mm locating head. Both values divide it exactly; 0.2 mm does not, and builds it 2.5 layers thick |
| Perimeters | **≥ 4**, and **solid** through every press fit | A bearing seat carries its load in hoop tension across a race only 2.5–7.0 mm wide ([007.1 §4](007.1-Parts-Catalog.md#4-bearings)). Infill reached by a press fit yields and the seat goes loose. No bearing bore, strake slot, or CF tube socket may have infill within 1.6 mm of its wall |
| Infill | **≥ 40 %** | Same reason, one step out: the printed bodies are scaffolding, but the fits in them are not |
| Orientation | Bearing-bore and strake-slot axes **parallel to Z** | A bore whose axis lies in the build plane is formed by stacked layers and stair-steps; the same bore built about Z is formed by in-plane perimeter motion, which is where the machine's accuracy is. Where two fits on one part disagree, the bearing seat wins — the strake slot is a bonded joint with an epoxy gap, the seat is not |
| Dimensional compensation | **Zero in the slicer** | The model carries the machine's fit in its two [print-fit clearances](#print-fits), tuned on the coupon below. A slicer hole/contour offset as well would count the machine's error twice |

### Print fits

Every printed part's `config="revised"` draws each mating feature at its mate's nominal size, less or more a
clearance per side from one library,
[`print_fit.scad`](../Hardware/Models/print_fit.scad):

| Class | Default, per side | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| **Press** | **0.05 mm** | A printed part against a bought part it is seated on, located by or bonded to: bearing seats and journals, the motor lugs, the circular spline's recess and step, the flexspline hub's spigot and nubs, and the bores, slots and spigots a CF rod, CF tube or strake is bonded to |
| **Slip** | **0.15 mm** | A printed part against another printed part, whatever the fit does: the Pivot Stator Holder's body in the Arm Body, the Ex Gear Stator Holder's core and keys in the External Gear, the Diff Keeper on Diff Body B's tube, and the Diff Shaft Pulley over the Diff Gear Shaft's band. And every fastener's clearance: screw holes, nut traps and pockets |
| **Running** | **0.50 mm** | Not a fit: a gap between two printed parts that turn past each other and must never touch, measured normal to the surface. `#730-002` Diff Body B against the toes of the three bevel crowns that turn in it — `#720-002`, `#720-001` and the Split Gear — each held off the volume its teeth sweep |

A fit's class follows its mate. A bought part is made to size, so a fit against it leaves room for one
printed surface's error; two printed parts each bring their own. A running gap also allows for the play
in the bearings that carry the moving part, so it is wider than either.

- A feature whose drawn gap already reaches its class's clearance keeps the gap it has.
- `config="previous"` draws no clearance, because it is the reference mesh its dist gate measures.
- Each part's header lists its fits and their classes. Each group's `render-all.rs` probes every listed
  fit for an open gap with a wall behind it, at whatever clearances `print_fit.scad` holds.
- **No clearance is drawn on:**
  - the [base mounting plate](007.1-Parts-Catalog.md#c-509--base-mounting-plate-stock), which is
    machined;
  - the glue rigs, whose registers are precision fits by design
    ([§ Tooling — glue rigs](#tooling--glue-rigs));
  - the Split Gear's brad holes, which are interference fits by design;
  - face and shoulder contacts;
  - the tool interface set, whose source is Fusion rather than OpenSCAD.

**The coupon that qualifies a machine** is
[`950-Tooling/fit_coupon.scad`](../Hardware/Models/950-Tooling/fit_coupon.scad). It carries each fit class
exactly as the parts draw it. It does not carry the running gap, because a gap whose surfaces never meet
is not a fit a machine can fail:
- an MR128 seat at the press clearance (the `SEAT_D` of
  [`720-001_DiffGearShaft.scad`](../Hardware/Models/700-Differential/720-001_DiffGearShaft.scad));
- `#710-001`'s slot for the [C-502](007.1-Parts-Catalog.md#c-502--carbon-fibre-strip-092--220) strake,
  at the press clearance;
- a Ø8 bore at the press clearance, for the
  [C-506](007.1-Parts-Catalog.md#c-506--carbon-fibre-round-tube-8-mm-od--6-mm-id) tube;
- a printed peg with a bore at the slip clearance.

A machine is qualified when:
- the bearing presses in without a reamer;
- the strake enters the slot, and the tube the bore, by hand, without rocking;
- the peg slides through the slip bore.

A fit that fails is corrected by editing its clearance in `print_fit.scad` and printing the coupon again,
never by sanding parts. The coupon carried a 1.45 × 4.50 mm slot until
[DC-11(e)](009-Design-Completion.md#procurement-data): that is the C-503 *strip's* section, not any slot
in the robot, and a slot cut to it could not be passed. Qualification is
[DC-11(d)](009-Design-Completion.md#procurement-data).

### Part identifiers

Three numbering schemes appear in this document, and only one names every part:

- **`PBS #`** — **the authoritative part number.** It is the only scheme covering all 70 printed parts, and
  the one [007](007-Bill-of-Materials.md), [007.1](007.1-Parts-Catalog.md), [008](008-Assembly.md) and the
  [`Hardware/Models/`](../Hardware/Models/README.md) file names all key on. A part number alone locates its
  file.
- **`HDI-` / `TI1-` CAD IDs** — body names in the GLTF model, given in the `CAD ID` column where known.
  They cover only 13 of 70 parts, so they identify a part but cannot enumerate the set.
- **`KP` / `KA` numbers** — the superseded **v1** design in
  [`Hardware/Models/Reference/onshape-v1/`](../Hardware/Models/Reference/onshape-v1/README.md). These belong
  to no current part and are reference geometry only.

### Tooling — glue rigs

The glue rigs ([007.1 subassembly](007-Bill-of-Materials.md#0071-glue-rig-assembly)) are epoxy jig tooling:
troughs that hold both ends of a bonded span at their finished spacing while the epoxy cures. Each rig end
carries the feature that locates the part it seats — the negative of its outline where a shell is what the
jig can hold, a spigot into its bore where the part offers one. **They are distinct geometry and need no
extra copies of any robot part** — the parts they hold are the robot's own. The two 6703 bearings
[007 § Aggregate](007-Bill-of-Materials.md#aggregate-hardware-quantities-whole-robot) carries for the rigs
are how the L3 rig registers on the differential (below).

### Model-vs-BOM discrepancies

Mismatches between the model set and [007](007-Bill-of-Materials.md) are under
[DC-11(e)](009-Design-Completion.md#procurement-data), which owns what each one is and what it would cost
to get wrong. None blocks a build. The one that affects printing — check it against the model set before you
start — is `HDI-311-006C_J2StatorHolderCap_ConeDrive`, a real second body of the ConeDrive stator holder
with no BOM row. The five `#720-005` CF strakes were listed here as a second such case until the
differential's parts were searched for the slot that would take one; the row is withdrawn.

**Two affect ordering rather than printing**, and both are bearing counts the differential was short of
against its own steps: MR128 by two and 6703 by one.
[007.6](007-Bill-of-Materials.md#0076-differential), [C-404](007.1-Parts-Catalog.md#4-bearings) and
[C-405](007.1-Parts-Catalog.md#4-bearings) are corrected, and
[DC-11(e)](009-Design-Completion.md#procurement-data) records how the MR128 count came to be re-derived
downward from a figure that was nearer right.

**`HDI-610-006_MotorShaftCoupler` is not `#630-004`.** The two geometries are different parts, and the CAD
name serves the assembly as a generic motor-shaft hub — the measurements and the instance-by-instance
account are in [DC-11(e)](009-Design-Completion.md#procurement-data). `#630-004` stays at quantity **3**,
one per strain-wave drive, and its row below drops the CAD ID rather than carry a mapping that does not
hold.

**J4's code disk is not a separate part, and no row is missing.** The 115-slot track
[003](003-Kinematics.md#joint-definitions) specifies is cut into the mating rim of `#730-002` Diff Body B
rather than onto a disk of its own, so the four disks in this list — J1, J2, J3 and J5 — are the complete
set ([DC-11(e)](009-Design-Completion.md#the-j4-code-disk-is-missing)).

## Regenerating this list

This document is derived. **Do not hand-edit quantities** — regenerate them.

### When to regenerate

Whenever [007](007-Bill-of-Materials.md) changes, or when the model archives are revised (a new Thingiverse
update, an OnShape release). A model revision that changes a part's geometry without changing its quantity
still requires the [Source](#model-file-sources) table to be re-checked.

### Procedure

1. **Extract every row typed `3D print`** from the subassembly tables in
   [007](007-Bill-of-Materials.md). Parse the tables rather than reading them by eye.

2. **Apply per-subassembly multipliers.**
   [007.3](007-Bill-of-Materials.md#0073-harmonic-drive-motors) is quoted *per motor and is built twice*.
   Its Wave Gen Coupler / Flex Spline Attach / Flex Spline Cap rows therefore contribute 2 each, which
   combine with [007.8](007-Bill-of-Materials.md#0078-external-gear)'s 1 to give 3 — one per strain-wave
   drive. Sanity-check group totals against the physical count of drives, joints, or connectors they serve.

3. **Resolve every non-numeric quantity.** [007](007-Bill-of-Materials.md) contains "as needed" and
   "per joint". A print list cannot carry those — resolve each to a number, state the rule that produced it
   (*one strain-relief pair per 6-pin connector*), and flag it if the rule is a judgement call.

4. **Separate tooling from robot structure.** Glue-rig and solder-jig parts are jigs, not robot structure.
   Carry them as a separate total so a build that skips the jigs does not over-print. Never fold them into
   the robot count.

5. **Reconcile against the CAD model.** Compare the extracted list to the `HDI-`/`TI1-` part names in
   `dde/HDIMeterModel.gltf` and the OnShape assembly. Record every part present in one and absent from the
   other under [Model-vs-BOM discrepancies](#model-vs-bom-discrepancies) — do not silently adopt either
   side. The GLTF is a kinematic-and-skin model, so absence from it is not by itself evidence a part does
   not exist.

6. **Re-check the [source archives](#model-file-sources).** Confirm each link resolves and note which
   archive currently holds each family. Where a part appears in both the HD set and the HD update, the
   **update supersedes**.

7. **Regenerate the `Model` column from
   [`PART-INDEX.md`](../Hardware/Models/PART-INDEX.md).** That index owns which file is which part; this
   column is generated from it and states no mapping of its own. Every row carries one, a part with no
   file is a defect in the mirror rather than an empty cell, and a part whose source of record is a
   `.scad` links the `.scad` and not a rendered mesh.

8. **Carry the `CAD ID` column forward.** It is the durable identifier; PBS numbers are legacy and contain
   known collisions (see [007.1 §6](007.1-Parts-Catalog.md#6-fasteners)). Fill in a `CAD ID` whenever one
   is recovered, and never delete one.

9. **Update the [totals](#totals)** and record the change in [CHANGES.md](../CHANGES.md).
