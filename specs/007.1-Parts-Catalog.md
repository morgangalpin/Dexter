# 007.1 — Parts Catalog and Sourcing

This document is the **de-duplicated purchase list for one complete robot**. Where
[007-Bill-of-Materials.md](007-Bill-of-Materials.md) lists parts *by subassembly* — so the same M3 nut
appears in six places — this document lists each distinct orderable item **exactly once**, with the full
product specification needed to order the correct item and at least one supplier link per part.

It is a **derived artifact**: it is generated from [007](007-Bill-of-Materials.md), which is itself derived
from the design in [004](004-Mechanical-Architecture.md)–[006](006-Firmware-and-Calibration.md). When the
design changes, regenerate both. The procedure is in
[Regenerating this catalog](#regenerating-this-catalog).

3D-printed parts are **not** listed here. They are in [007.2-Printed-Parts.md](007.2-Printed-Parts.md).

Status markers per [README.md](README.md#design-status).

## How to use this catalog

**Order of operations.** Lead time, not cost, drives the sequence:

1. **[C-201](#2-strain-wave-drive-components) strain-wave component sets** — 9–12 week lead time, special
   order, and the highest-risk item in the build. Start vendor contact *before anything else*
   ([DC-1](009-Design-Completion.md#strain-wave-component-set)).
2. **[C-701](#7-electronics-and-wiring) MicroZed** and the fabricated PCBs — board-house turnaround.
3. **[C-501](#5-structural-composites-and-metal-stock) carbon fiber** — but do not *cut* it until
   [DC-5](009-Design-Completion.md#link-member-lengths) confirms the L2/L3 socket seat depth.
4. **[C-301](#3-belts-and-pulleys) wrist driven pulleys** — do not order until
   [DC-3](009-Design-Completion.md#wrist-reduction-ratio) fixes the tooth counts. The 16T *motor* pulleys
   ([C-303](#3-belts-and-pulleys)) are settled and may be ordered now.
5. Everything else is commodity stock, orderable at any time.

**Quantities carry no slack.** Every quantity below is the exact design requirement. Order fasteners,
bearings, and carbon fiber in excess — printed threads strip, strakes snap, and M2 hardware is easy to lose.

**Supplier links.** Each part gives suppliers in this order of preference:

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `CA` | Canadian supplier — domestic shipping, no cross-border brokerage or duty |
| `US` | United States supplier — used when no Canadian source stocks the part |
| `INT` | Elsewhere (China, UK, EU) — used when neither of the above stocks the part |

Two kinds of link appear, and the difference matters:

- A **direct product link** points at one specific orderable SKU. Ordering that SKU gives the correct part.
- A **search link** (marked *search*) points at a supplier's search results for the exact trade
  designation. These are used for commodity items — bearings, fasteners — where the designation
  (`6703-2RS`, `M3×12 DIN 912`) fully determines the part and every supplier stocks several equivalent
  brands. Pick any result matching the specification column.

**Prices are not recorded here.** They move; the specification does not. Links resolve to current pricing.

### C-101 — NEMA-17 stepper, 0.9°/step

**Specification.** This is the part most easily ordered wrong: "NEMA 17" is a *frame size only* and over a
hundred motors share it. All of the following are required:

| Property | Requirement | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Step angle | **0.9° (400 full steps/rev)** — *not* 1.8° | `AxisCal` assumes 400 steps/rev ([006](006-Firmware-and-Calibration.md#drive-constants-axiscal)). A 1.8° motor halves every joint's resolution and doubles its commanded travel |
| Frame | NEMA 17 (42 × 42 mm), 31 mm bolt circle, 4 × M3 face holes | Printed Motor End Cap and Flex Spline Attach interfaces |
| Winding | Bipolar, 4-lead | A4983 drivers are bipolar-only ([005](005-Electronics-and-Control.md#boards)) |
| Rated current | **≤ 2.0 A/phase** | A4983 channels are fused at 3.0 A; ≈2 A/phase is the design point |
| Holding torque | **≥ 0.45 N·m** | Legacy build record specifies 0.52 N·m — see the note below |
| Shaft | **Ø5 mm, ≥ 24 mm long, with a flat (D-cut)** | The Wave Gen Coupler is reamed onto the shaft; [008.9](008-Assembly.md) tightens a pulley set screw "against the flat of each motor shaft" |
| Body length | ≤ 48 mm | Envelope inside the Motor End Cap |

`[Provisional]` — **the original equipment part number is not in the design record.** The legacy parts
list gives only *"Stepper Motors, 25 mm shaft, 0.9°, 0.52 N·m"*. The recommended qualifying part below is
0.46 N·m with a 24 mm shaft, which meets every requirement above but is not a proven identity match.
**Confirm body length and shaft length against the printed Motor End Cap before ordering all five.**
Tracked as [DC-11](009-Design-Completion.md#procurement-data).

⚠️ **Applied Motion HT17-271 does not qualify.** It is a **1.8°** motor. The whole HT17-2xx series is 1.8°.
Any 0.9° NEMA 17 that meets the table above is acceptable; step angle is the non-negotiable property.

**Recommended part: StepperOnline `17HM19-2004S`** — 0.9°, bipolar 4-wire, 46 N·cm, 2.0 A/phase, 2.8 V,
1.4 Ω, 3.0 mH, 42 × 42 × 48 mm, Ø5 × 24 mm shaft with 15 mm D-cut, 370 g.

- `CA` — [StepperOnline Canada — 17HM19-2004S](https://www.stepperonline.ca/nema-17-bipolar-0-9deg-46ncm-65-1oz-in-2a-2-9v-42x42x48mm-4-wires-17hm19-2004s.html)
- `CA` — [SPOOL3D (Calgary) — NEMA17 0.9° 48 mm](https://spool3d.ca/nema17-0-9-degree-stepper-motor-48mm/)
- `CA` — [Canada Robotix — NEMA 17 400 steps/rev](https://www.canadarobotix.com/products/786) *(1.7 A, 48 N·cm — verify shaft length)*
- `US` — [StepperOnline — 17HM19-2004S](https://www.omc-stepperonline.com/nema-17-bipolar-0-9deg-46ncm-65-1oz-in-2a-2-9v-42x42x48mm-4-wires-17hm19-2004s)

### C-103 — Power supply, 36 V DC

**Specification.** **36 V DC, ≥ 4 A (≈144 W)** external desktop/laptop-style brick, with a DC barrel plug
matching [C-712](#7-electronics-and-wiring).

**Substitution limits are safety-relevant and are specified in
[005 § Power](005-Electronics-and-Control.md#power):**

- **36 V is a ceiling-constrained choice, not a preference.** The board's `LTC3786` boost controller sets a
  **38 V absolute input limit**. Do not exceed it.
- **Under-voltage is a failure mode, not a slowdown.** A 12 V or 24 V brick makes the arm grind and buzz,
  stall mid-motion, and fail to find home. Do not substitute one.

**Recommended part: MEAN WELL `GST160A36-R7B`** — 36 V, 4.44 A, 160 W desktop adapter, IEC C14 inlet.
Confirm the DC plug on the variant you order matches the robot's power connector, or fit the connector
yourself with [C-711](#7-electronics-and-wiring)/[C-712](#7-electronics-and-wiring).

- `CA` / `US` — [Digi-Key — GST160A36-R7B](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/mean-well-usa-inc/GST160A36-R7B/10659701) *(Digi-Key Canada carries the same part number and ships domestically)*
- `US` — [Jameco — GST160A36-R7B](https://www.jameco.com/z/GST160A36-R7B-MEAN-WELL-160W-36V-4-44A-AC-DC-High-Reliability-Industrial-Desktop-Power-Supply_2683431.html)

**Belt construction.** The legacy parts list specifies *"timing belts, fibreglass reinforced"*. Fibreglass
or Kevlar cords are required — the low stretch is what keeps the J4/J5 belt stages inside the FPGA's
correction band ([005](005-Electronics-and-Control.md#control-architecture)). Plain rubber belts will
creep.

**Sizes are non-standard for 3D-printer stock.** 900 mm and 1120 mm × 6 mm are stocked by most GT2
vendors; **1320 mm × 9 mm is not commonly stocked** and may need a custom weld — RobotDigg makes GT2
endless belts to length in 6 and 9 mm. Order these early.

⚠️ **Driven pulleys are not in this catalog.** The J4/J5 driven pulleys are printed
([007.2](007.2-Printed-Parts.md)), and their tooth counts are the open half of
[DC-3](009-Design-Completion.md#wrist-reduction-ratio). The net wrist reduction must be **13.5:1**; the
previous version's 90T/16T stage gives only 5.625:1, a 2.4× error. Do not commit belt lengths that assume
the old pulley set without re-checking the net ratio.

## Regenerating this catalog

This document is derived. **Do not hand-edit quantities** — regenerate them, or the two documents drift.

### When to regenerate

Whenever [007](007-Bill-of-Materials.md) changes: a part added, removed, re-quantified, or re-specified.
[007](007-Bill-of-Materials.md) itself is regenerated when the design in
[004](004-Mechanical-Architecture.md)–[006](006-Firmware-and-Calibration.md) changes.

### Procedure

1. **Extract every row** from the subassembly tables in [007](007-Bill-of-Materials.md).
   The tables are regular Markdown: `| PBS # | Part | Type | Qty | Notes |`. Parse rather than read by eye —
   the discrepancies in [Corrections to 007](#corrections-to-007) were all eye-count errors.

2. **Apply per-subassembly multipliers.** This is the step that goes wrong most often.
   [007.3](007-Bill-of-Materials.md#0073-harmonic-drive-motors) is quoted **per motor and is built twice** —
   multiply every quantity in it by 2. Check each subassembly heading for a similar note before summing.

3. **Aggregate by orderable identity, not by PBS number.** Two PBS numbers in
   [007](007-Bill-of-Materials.md) each cover two different sizes (see the warning in
   [§6](#6-fasteners)). Group by *what you would type into a supplier's search box* — "M2 × 12 mm socket
   head cap screw" — and treat the PBS number as traceability metadata only.

4. **Separate tooling from robot structure.** Rows in
   [7.1 Glue Rig](007-Bill-of-Materials.md#0071-glue-rig-assembly) duplicate parts listed in their real
   subassemblies. Carry them as an explicit `(+n tooling)` suffix, never folded into the robot total.

5. **Route printed parts to [007.2](007.2-Printed-Parts.md)**, fabricated stock to
   [§5](#5-structural-composites-and-metal-stock), and everything else to its category here.

6. **Convert cut pieces into stock quantities.** For each cut item, sum `length × count`, add ~1.5 mm kerf
   per cut, then divide by the supplier's stock length and round **up**. State both the cut schedule and
   the stock count — a builder needs the first to cut and the second to order.

7. **Re-verify every supplier link.** Links rot; DragonPlate's `pID=` scheme going dead is exactly this
   failure. For each link, confirm it resolves and that the product still matches the specification column.
   Replace dead links rather than deleting the part.

8. **Preserve catalog IDs.** `C-xxx` IDs are stable across revisions and are referenced from
   [008](008-Assembly.md) and [009](009-Design-Completion.md). Retire an ID rather than reusing it for a
   different part.

9. **Reconcile with [007](007-Bill-of-Materials.md).** Any total that disagrees is a defect in one document
   or the other. Fix the source, note it in [Corrections to 007](#corrections-to-007), and record the change
   in [CHANGES.md](../CHANGES.md).

### Sourcing rules

- **At least one Canadian supplier per part** where one exists; then US; then elsewhere. Tag every link
  `CA`/`US`/`INT`.
- **Prefer a direct product link.** Fall back to a supplier search link only for commodity parts whose
  trade designation fully determines the item, and mark it *(search)*.
- **Specify the part, not the brand.** Give the properties that make a substitute acceptable, so a builder
  facing a stock-out can judge alternatives. Where a property is non-negotiable, say why — the 0.9° step
  angle in [C-101](#c-101--nema-17-stepper-09step) and the 38 V ceiling in
  [C-103](#c-103--power-supply-36-v-dc) are the model.
- **Record no prices.** They move; the specification does not.
- **Mark provenance honestly.** If a part number is recommended rather than proven against the original
  build, say so and mark it `[Provisional]` with a [009](009-Design-Completion.md) reference.
