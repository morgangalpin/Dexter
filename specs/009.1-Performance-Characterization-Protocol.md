# 009.1 — Performance Characterization Protocol

This document specifies how the first physical build is measured: the instruments, the procedure for each
measurement, the rule that decides its result, and where that result is written. It is the instrument
[DC-9](009-Design-Completion.md#performance-characterization) calls for, and carries no design status of
its own — [009](009-Design-Completion.md) owns that.

**No value here is a design value.** Every figure a measurement is compared against is owned by another
document and linked, never restated. Where a requirement is marked *to be characterized* in
[002](002-Requirements.md#3-precision-and-performance-requirements), this protocol produces the value rather
than testing against one: an acceptance threshold invented here would become the answer the build was run
to find.

## Preconditions

The unit these tests run on — what it contains, what it leaves out, and the order it is built in — is
specified in [009.2](009.2-Test-Build-Manifest.md).

| Condition | Source |
|---|---|
| The build is complete through [008.12](008-Assembly.md#00812-firmware-configuration-and-calibration) | [008](008-Assembly.md#assembly-order) |
| The power-on test has passed on this board and harness | [DC-7](009-Design-Completion.md#motor-control-pcb) |
| Factory calibration is complete — required by every check that reads encoder counts | [006 § Step 3](006-Firmware-and-Calibration.md#step-3--movement-calibration-and-go-live) |
| `LinkLengths` carries the specified L4 before any Cartesian move | [006](006-Firmware-and-Calibration.md#firmware-defaults-defaultsmake_ins) |
| The robot has clearance for full rotation on every joint, and no person is within reach while it moves | [006 § Step 2](006-Firmware-and-Calibration.md#step-2--per-joint-eye-calibration) |

⚠️ **J4 has no mechanical end stop.** Its 115-slot track runs the full 360° and no part limits its travel
([DC-11(e)](009-Design-Completion.md#the-j4-code-disk-is-missing)), so the firmware boundaries are the only
limit J4 has. Confirm `J4BoundryHigh`/`J4BoundryLow` are loaded
([006](006-Firmware-and-Calibration.md#firmware-defaults-defaultsmake_ins)) before commanding J4 at all.

**Two measurements are taken during assembly, not after.** The base and the wrist each come apart once.

| Measurement | Taken at | Specified in |
|---|---|---|
| Mounting face to J2 axis, with the clamp count | [008.4](008-Assembly.md#0084-main-pivot) step 18 | [§ 3.1](#test-31-mounting-face-to-j2-axis) |
| Belt centre distance, both stages | [008.9](008-Assembly.md#0089-belts), before ordering belts | [§ 2.2](#test-22-belt-centre-distances) |

**Order.** Section 1 runs on the differential before the covers close. Section 3.1 runs at base assembly.
Everything else runs on the calibrated, fully assembled robot, in section order: section 3.2 fixes the
base, section 4 loads it, and section 5 reads a rail while the arm runs.

## Instruments

| Instrument | Resolution required | Used by |
|---|---|---|
| Digital or dial indicator on a magnetic base | 0.001 mm (0.01 mm minimum) | [§ 4.2](#test-42-positioning-repeatability) |
| Digital caliper | 0.01 mm | [§ 1](#section-1-differential-first-build-checklist), [§ 2.2](#test-22-belt-centre-distances) |
| Height gauge or depth caliper, 250 mm range | 0.02 mm | [§ 3.1](#test-31-mounting-face-to-j2-axis) |
| Steel rule or tape, 1 m | 1 mm | [§ 4.1](#test-41-maximum-reach-and-envelope) |
| Torque wrench, 2–20 N·m | 0.5 N·m | [§ 3.2](#test-32-base-rigidity-under-dynamic-load) |
| Digital multimeter | 10 mV | [§ 5](#section-5-fan-rail-measurement) |
| Bench supply with a current display, or an inline DC ammeter | 10 mA | [§ 4.3](#test-43-rated-payload) |
| Calibrated masses, 0.25 kg steps to 2 kg | 1 % | [§ 4.3](#test-43-rated-payload) |
| Logged command timestamps, or a stopwatch | 0.1 s | [§ 4.4](#test-44-maximum-speed) |

The indicator resolution follows from what is being measured: REQ-PRE-4 predicts a tip resolution of
≈ 3 µm at 0.5 m ([002](002-Requirements.md#3-precision-and-performance-requirements)), and an instrument that
cannot resolve 10 µm cannot measure repeatability on this arm at all. A mark on a board or a photograph of
the tool tip resolves nothing finer than about 1 mm and does not substitute.

## Section 1: Differential First-Build Checklist

This section discharges the physical half of the [DC-2](009-Design-Completion.md#differential-detail-design)
contract. Every check is against the differential as [008.6](008-Assembly.md#0086-differential) builds it,
before the covers close.

**A failure here is a print-fit result before it is a design result.** Press interference and bevel
backlash are parameters in
[`diff_params.scad`](../Hardware/Models/700-Differential/diff_params.scad); re-print at an adjusted value
and repeat the check before concluding anything about the design.

### Test 1.1: J4 and J5 travel

Travel limits are [003 § Joint travel limits](003-Kinematics.md#joint-travel-limits). Run the check in two
stages — by hand first, because a mechanism that binds under power has already been driven into the
binding.

1. **By hand, unpowered.** With the differential assembled and before the belts go on
   ([008.9](008-Assembly.md#0089-belts)), turn each of J4 and J5 by hand through its full travel and back.
   Resistance is even across the range. A tight spot, a grinding feel, or a ratcheting step is a fault.

2. **Under power, joint by joint.** Move with the `a` oplet
   ([003 § Motion commands](003-Kinematics.md#motion-commands)), which is coordinated and ramped and needs
   no calibration. Command J4 to 0°, then in 10° steps to ±100°, then to each boundary — **23 positions**.
   Command J5 to 0°, then in 10° steps to ±190° — **39 positions**. Hold 0.5 s at each. At every position
   the joint arrives and holds, nothing stalls, and no grinding is audible.

3. **Both outputs together.** Command the four corners of the travel rectangle and the four edge
   midpoints, holding 1 s at each. The corners are the differential's worst case: the bevel set runs at its
   greatest relative displacement when the two outputs sit at opposite extremes.

**Result:** pass when every commanded position is reached and held, at every stage, with no stall and no
grinding. Encoder agreement at the count level is not tested here —
[§ 4.2](#test-42-positioning-repeatability) measures that on a calibrated robot.

### Test 1.2: Tool conductors through the wrist

The six conductors are fed through Diff Body B at [008.6](008-Assembly.md#0086-differential) step 14, three
per channel, and land on `J20`, `J24` and `J25`
([005 § Tool interface wiring](005-Electronics-and-Control.md#tool-interface-wiring)).

⚠️ **J5 does not rotate continuously.** Its travel is bounded at ±190°
([003](003-Kinematics.md#joint-travel-limits)), so the cycle below is a sweep and return — 380° of travel
per pass — not a revolution.

1. With the bundle dressed as [008.6](008-Assembly.md#0086-differential) leaves it, sweep J5 from one
   boundary to the other and back, **10 passes**.
2. At each boundary, confirm the tool responds — a servo query over the data bus is sufficient
   ([005 § Command interface](005-Electronics-and-Control.md#command-interface)).
3. Inspect the conductors where they leave the shaft at both ends: no abraded or cut insulation, no
   conductor drawn taut, no wrap around a standoff.
4. Pull the bundle at the tool end with ≈ 5 N. It does not move at its strain relief, and no encoder
   reading changes.

**Result:** pass when the insulation is undamaged, the tool answers at both boundaries, and the bundle is
retained. Damage found at one shaft exit only indicates a dressing fault; damage at both indicates the bore
path.

### Test 1.3: Code disks

J4 is read off the 115-slot track cut into `#730-002`'s rim and J5 off `#710-004`, through the Angle and
Rotate shrouds ([004 § Differential interface](004-Mechanical-Architecture.md#differential-interface)).
The instrument is the calibration run itself — this test reads its output for the two wrist joints rather
than repeating a procedure [006](006-Firmware-and-Calibration.md#step-3--movement-calibration-and-go-live)
owns.

1. **From `Check_Eye_Order`:** the J4 and J5 rows each show the clear largest-value pattern.
2. **From `Initial_Scan`:** index-pulse spike separation increases by 2 on each side of the home spikes,
   with no gap inside usable travel, on both wrist joints.
3. **Sweep.** After calibration, move each of J4 and J5 slowly across full travel and back while logging
   the encoder count. The count changes monotonically with commanded direction, no slot is skipped or
   counted twice, and the return reads the start value.

**Result:** pass when both joints calibrate without the opto blocks being revisited and both sweeps are
monotonic. **If J4 fails where J5 passes, suspect the track before the opto block:** J4's slots are cut in
a printed rim rather than a separate disk, so their edges carry that rim's print quality.

**Record:** `J4_track_sweep.csv`, `J5_disk_sweep.csv` — commanded angle and raw encoder count per step.

## Section 2: Wrist Station — L4 Measurement

### Test 2.1: L4 from Cartesian residual

**L4 cannot be measured with a caliper.** The J4 and J5 axes **intersect**
([004 § Differential interface](004-Mechanical-Architecture.md#differential-interface)), so there is no
axis-to-axis distance on the built arm to put an instrument across. L4 is the along-arm separation of two
kinematic stations in a five-length model that does not carry the across-arm offset between them
([003 § Link lengths](003-Kinematics.md#link-lengths)), and a quantity of that kind is observable only
through the kinematics it feeds. That is also why the measurement waits for calibration instead of being
taken while the wrist is open.

The observable is Cartesian: an error in L4 displaces every commanded Cartesian position, while `a` and `P`
joint moves are unaffected ([006](006-Firmware-and-Calibration.md#firmware-defaults-defaultsmake_ins)).

1. **Fix a datum.** Mount the indicator from [§ 4.2](#test-42-positioning-repeatability) on a fixed base
   and a target feature at the tool interface.
2. **Pose set.** Choose at least six poses that hold J1, J2 and J3 constant and differ only in J4 and J5,
   spanning each wrist joint's travel. Avoid singular poses
   ([003 § Inverse kinematics](003-Kinematics.md#inverse-kinematics)).
3. **Command and measure.** Command each pose with `M` and measure the actual tip position against the
   datum in all three axes. The set of differences between commanded and measured position is the residual.
4. **Solve.** Repeat the pose set with the `LinkLengths` L4 field set to the specified value and to values
   on either side of it, rewriting the firmware line between runs. The value that minimizes the residual
   is the built arm's L4.

**Result:** the minimizing L4, the residual at that value, and the residual at the specified value. A
residual that stays large at every L4 is the across-arm offset showing itself, which is what
[003 § Link lengths](003-Kinematics.md#link-lengths) records as the cost of the five-length model; report
it as measured rather than adjusting L4 to absorb it.

### Test 2.2: Belt centre distances

Belt lengths are back-solved from the previous version's as-built belts and are confirmed against the built
centre distance before ordering ([007.1 § 3](007.1-Parts-Catalog.md#3-belts-and-pulleys)). Neither path's
centre distance equals its link length, so the figure must come off the build.

For each of the two stages, with the pulleys mounted and before the belt is fitted, measure the distance
between the two pulley axes. Compare against the centre distance
[007.1 § 3](007.1-Parts-Catalog.md#3-belts-and-pulleys) back-solves for that stage.

**Result:** both measured centre distances. These belong to
[DC-3](009-Design-Completion.md#wrist-reduction-ratio); the pulleys they run on are re-cut under
[DC-12](009-Design-Completion.md#wrist-pulley-rework), so take this measurement on the re-cut parts.

## Section 3: Base First-Build Checklist

### Test 3.1: Mounting face to J2 axis

[DC-13](009-Design-Completion.md#base-height-and-l1) turns on which clamp count the authoritative L1
belongs to, and states why a height on its own settles nothing. The measurement is therefore two
readings, not one, and both are taken at
[008.4](008-Assembly.md#0084-main-pivot) step 18 while the stack is open.

1. **Count the clamps** installed under the Main Pivot.
2. **Measure** from the bottom face of the Base Mounting Plate vertically to the J2 axis, with the arm
   upright. Take the J2 axis at the pivot bore centre, averaging readings to both bore edges.

The three candidate values — as modelled, as designed, and L1 itself — are tabulated in
[DC-13](009-Design-Completion.md#base-height-and-l1), separated by 4.000 mm and 11.000 mm. An instrument
good to ±0.5 mm distinguishes them; no tolerance band is stated here, because the reading is the answer
rather than a test against one.

**Result:** the height in millimetres and the clamp count. Both are needed: the reconciliation
[DC-13](009-Design-Completion.md#base-height-and-l1) calls for cannot be done from the height alone.

### Test 3.2: Base rigidity under dynamic load

REQ-ENV-5 requires the mounted plate to react the arm's full dynamic load
([002](002-Requirements.md#7-environmental-and-operational-requirements)). The plate is sized against a
calculated overturning moment that an unbolted plate cannot resist
([004 § Base mounting plate](004-Mechanical-Architecture.md#base-mounting-plate)), so **this test checks
the bolting, not the plate.**

1. **Mount** per [008.2](008-Assembly.md#0082-base) step 5 — through the four corner holes, or the
   equivalent T-slot clamping. The fastener is specified in
   [004 § Base mounting plate](004-Mechanical-Architecture.md#base-mounting-plate).
2. **Torque** each of the four uniformly and record the value. For the specified M6 in class 8.8, 10 N·m
   is ≈ 70 % of proof load at a nominal friction coefficient; reduce it if the work surface cannot carry
   that. Confirm no gap between the plate and the work surface after torquing.
3. **Datum.** Record the distance from a fixed reference on the work surface to one plate corner.
4. **Static case.** Extend the arm horizontally at full reach carrying the largest mass that passed
   [§ 4.3](#test-43-rated-payload), and hold 10 s.
5. **Dynamic case.** Run five cycles of home → full reach → home → opposite full reach → home at the
   configured speed and acceleration ([§ 4.4](#test-44-maximum-speed)).
6. **Re-measure** the corner datum, and re-check the torque on all four fasteners.

**Result:** pass when the corner datum has not moved, no fastener has lost torque, and nothing creaks or
cracks under load. Record the datum before and after, and the torque before and after, for each of the four
positions.

⚠️ Run this test after [§ 4.3](#test-43-rated-payload), not before: it needs the payload that section
measures, and it loads the base harder than anything else in this protocol.

## Section 4: Performance Envelope

These four measurements produce the values REQ-WS-6, REQ-WS-8 and REQ-PRE-5/6/7 are waiting for
([002](002-Requirements.md#3-precision-and-performance-requirements)). None of them has a pass mark: the
measurement *is* the requirement's value.

### Test 4.1: Maximum reach and envelope

**Datum.** The J1 axis passes through the centre of the robot-side bolt pattern, whose coordinates are
given from that axis ([004 § Base mounting plate](004-Mechanical-Architecture.md#base-mounting-plate));
scribe or otherwise mark that centre on the plate.

1. **Reach.** For each of the eight configuration combinations — the three booleans of
   [003 § Inverse kinematics](003-Kinematics.md#inverse-kinematics) — extend the arm to its furthest
   reachable pose and measure the horizontal distance from the datum to the tool tip.
2. **Envelope.** At a series of heights above the work surface, find the near and far reach boundary in the
   vertical plane and record the pair. The result is a measured side-view profile to set beside the
   profiles [003 § Workspace envelope](003-Kinematics.md#workspace-envelope) refers to.
3. **Physical limits.** At each boundary pose, record whether the limit is the joint boundary, a
   singularity, self-collision, or cable strain. Only the last two are new information from the build; the
   first two the model already predicts.

**Result:** the largest measured reach, the boundary profile, and the limiting cause at each boundary.
These become REQ-WS-6 and REQ-WS-8.

### Test 4.2: Positioning repeatability

Repeatability is the spread of the tip position on repeated returns to one pose. It is **not** accuracy,
and it is not derivable from encoder resolution: the encoder measures the joint, and everything between the
joint and the tip — belt tension, printed-part compliance, bearing clearance — lands in this number and
nowhere else.

1. **Target pose.** A non-singular mid-range pose with the tool interface accessible to an indicator.
2. **Setup.** Magnetic base on the work surface, indicator probing a target feature at the tool interface.
3. **Unidirectional cycle.** Command away from the pose and back, always approaching from the same
   direction, and read the indicator at rest. Repeat **30 cycles**; 10 is the minimum that yields a usable
   standard deviation.
4. **Repeat per axis.** Re-orient the indicator and repeat for each of the three axes.
5. **Bidirectional cycle.** Repeat 10 cycles approaching alternately from opposite directions. The
   difference between the unidirectional and bidirectional spreads is drivetrain backlash reaching the tip.

**Calculation.** Following the ISO 9283 convention for pose repeatability, report the mean distance from
the barycentre of the returned positions plus three standard deviations of that distance. Report the
unidirectional and bidirectional figures separately.

**Result:** repeatability in millimetres per axis, and the combined figure. This becomes REQ-PRE-5. Set
beside REQ-PRE-4's predicted tip resolution, the ratio between the two measures what the structure and
drivetrain cost.

### Test 4.3: Rated payload

⚠️ **Safety.** A stalling joint drops its load. Keep the sweep clear of people, and put a soft surface
under the tool before adding any mass.

**Bound the ladder below 2.5 kg.** Motor stall torque through the 52:1 base reduction
([C-101](007.1-Parts-Catalog.md#c-101--nema-17-stepper-09step),
[004 § Base joints](004-Mechanical-Architecture.md#base-joints-j1j3-strain-wave-drive)) is the ceiling at
J2, and the moving links above J2 ([003 § Link masses](003-Kinematics.md#link-masses-for-dynamics)) already
consume a substantial fraction of it at full extension. The limit is expected in the low kilograms.

1. Starting at 0 kg, secure a mass at the tool tip and command the arm to a mid-range pose, then to full
   horizontal reach. Hold 10 s at each.
2. At each mass record: whether every joint holds position, whether either move stalled or timed out, and
   the total supply current at rest and during the move.
3. Add 0.25 kg and repeat.

**Stop conditions.** Any one of: a joint does not hold position under load; a move stalls or times out; the
total supply current reaches the supply's rating ([005 § Power](005-Electronics-and-Control.md#power)).

**Result:** the largest mass that cleared every stop condition, and the current-versus-mass series. This
becomes REQ-PRE-6, and [§ 3.2](#test-32-base-rigidity-under-dynamic-load) loads the base with it.

### Test 4.4: Maximum speed

The parameters that bound speed, and the units their `S` arguments take, are specified in
[006 § Motion shaping parameters](006-Firmware-and-Calibration.md#motion-shaping-parameters). Record the
values in force before starting — the argument is not in degrees or arcseconds, and a speed test run
against a misread parameter measures nothing.

1. **Per joint.** For each of J1–J5, command a move of known angular extent across mid-range and time it
   from command to completion. Achieved rate is extent ÷ time, less the ramp.
2. **Against the limit.** Compare each achieved rate against the configured `MaxSpeed` converted to
   degrees per second. A joint that cannot reach its configured limit is reporting a mechanical or torque
   limit, not a software one.
3. **Coordinated.** Time a full point-to-point move, home → maximum reach → home.
4. **Straight-line.** Time a `T` move over a known Cartesian distance and compare the achieved tip speed
   against the configured `CartesianSpeed`.
5. **Under load.** Repeat steps 1 and 3 with the largest mass from [§ 4.3](#test-43-rated-payload).

**Result:** achieved rate per joint, achieved tip speed, and the same figures under load. These become
REQ-PRE-7. Raise the configured limits only after the arm meets the ones it has.

## Section 5: Fan Rail Measurement

[DC-7](009-Design-Completion.md#motor-control-pcb) criterion 3 is a reading, and
[DC-11(b)](009-Design-Completion.md#procurement-data) is what consumes it: the fan cannot be selected until
the rail that feeds it is known.

`J23` is the fan connector, fused by `F7`, and the schematic annotates its load rather than its source
([005 § Boards](005-Electronics-and-Control.md#boards)). **No expected voltage is stated here.** The
reading is the answer, and an expectation written down in advance is the error this measurement exists to
prevent.

1. With no fan connected, identify pin 1 and record the polarity across `J23`.
2. Measure the open-circuit voltage with the board idle and again while the arm runs a slow continuous
   cycle.
3. If a bench fan is available, repeat under load and record the sag.

**Result:** the voltage and polarity at `J23`, idle and running. That value fixes the rated voltage of
[C-716](007.1-Parts-Catalog.md#7-electronics-and-wiring).

Each such edit is a change to the design of record and is recorded as a change request in
[CHANGES.md](../CHANGES.md), in the format

protocol — a procedure that proved ambiguous, an instrument that was missing, a tolerance that proved
unusable — are made here and recorded the same way, so the second build runs an improved procedure.
